## TURNTALE 0.3.7 — 2026-10-23

- Replaces the immediate shedding heuristic with a shared strategic policy in `lib/bot.ts`, used by both practice opponents and automatic online turns. Legal play, accessible draw and empty-floor pass actions compete in the same evaluation. A frozen 0.3.6 policy lives only in `tests/fixtures/bot-v036.ts` for comparisons and is not bundled into the app.
- Preserves hand partitions and control entries: evaluates remaining groups, isolated weak tiles, opponent counters, urgent finishes and taking a public tile to build a future finish. Immediate wins and genuinely unanswerable control followed by a whole-hand finish are recognized first. The red-two example now acquires the stopper instead of blindly playing 10.
- Uses deterministic public-information sampling without replacement (10 worlds for two players, 8 for three/four). Candidate actions are followed through 12/16/20 subsequent individual turns; every simulated actor uses its own hand and public information. Mean outcome, downside variation, lead ownership and remaining combination quality select the action. Root candidates include multiple plays, draws, every legal size, the strongest single and an accessible red two even when its immediate shedding score is poor.
- Fully public small endgames (up to 15 remaining hand/floor tiles and two exposed floor tiles) use complete search: alpha-beta in two-player games and selfish-opponent MaxN in multiplayer games. Deck elimination may establish the final unknown hand only when all unseen cards must belong there and no floor slot is concealed. Exhausted search budgets fall back to estimation, never to an asserted proof. Normal lookahead is bounded; hands over 20 tiles use the public heuristic, and combinatorial candidate scoring is capped while retaining each family. This is stronger bounded play, not a guarantee of the globally optimal move under hidden information.
- Tests include the reproduced losing legacy move versus winning red-two acquisition, independent exhaustive winner checks using real `act()` transitions for 24 public endgames, immediate wins, avoiding gifted opponent finishes, straight/full-house preservation, a necessary high-pair defense, hidden-hand/covered-floor invariance, deterministic input-order-independent decisions, nonmutation and deck-elimination inference. Existing rules, timing, selection, leaderboard, privacy and online-timeout tests pass.
- Test suite scheduling: older 100/210-round random soak loops exceeded the 120-second per-process runner limit with deeper search. Normal rules tests now use 12 seeded games across all table sizes; multiplayer and takeover tests retain every assertion with smaller default matrices, expandable through `BOT_STRESS_ROUNDS`. The separate reproducible comparison benchmark exercises additional full games, rotating the improved bot through every seat on identical deals. The old unconditional high-pair expectation now permits preserving that pair for a later entry; a separate public tactical case requires the high pair when the low pair permits an immediate opposing finish.
- All tests use production rules with no browser access or private opponent hand access. Browser/device animation responsiveness was not measured; decision timings below are from the local Node runtime.
- Final comparison: 12 fixed deals per table size, improved bot rotated through every seat against 0.3.6 bots (108 full games). Two-player: 15/24 wins (62.5%), mean point delta −0.75; three-player: 25/36 (69.4%), +5.33; four-player: 26/48 (54.2%), +2.63. Equal-strength win shares would be 50% / 33.3% / 25%. This fixed sample supports improved win rate, not universal superiority; two-player average score remains a tradeoff to improve separately. Decision means were 42/53/52ms, p95 97/118/141ms, maximum 298ms in local Node; real mobile performance is unverified. A separate 48-tile-hand probe returned a legal bounded heuristic choice in 329ms.
