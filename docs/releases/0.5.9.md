# 0.5.9

Character conversation is a nonblocking overlay. Normal-mode Korean opponents have individual lines for ordinary plays, strong plays, being beaten, drawing, winning and waiting. All 30 challenge characters have individual defeat and waiting responses, with their existing character quotes used for confident plays. Reactions use submitted tiles and public events only; a concealed draw cannot change the line. A human turn waiting 18 seconds can receive one playful reminder. Repeated routine lines have a 15-second cooldown. Conversations do not extend reviews, turns or AI delays and never intercept input.

Fresh game sequence: shuffle at the seated angle, arrange/deal at that same angle and distance, zoom once, then lift to the overhead live board at constant scale. The same camera function drives DOM and GPU rendering. Physical bodies remain until the end of the lift. Removed the additional 3D room geometry and stand-in character figures from the entrance; the background plate stays stable and matches the in-game environment. Existing tile textures and threefold physical thickness are retained.

Resume mounts the actual saved board immediately with an 850 ms opacity fade. It does not mount GameEntrance, initialize its GPU renderer, plan a shuffle, re-deal cards or move a camera. Saved hand, table, score and opponent identities are preserved. Reduced motion skips the fade. Load failures release the entrance lock.

Opponent names and scores share a horizontal row; the larger score aligns right. Own score has a persistent badge beside the hand controls, independent of the changing turn message.

Validation: TypeScript and production build; character event/privacy/idle thresholds; entrance order, camera seams, zoom/lift separation and physical-depth handoff; 12 shuffle simulations including interpolated collision checks; all 60 deal bodies for 2–4 players, portrait/landscape and 0/±90° layouts; layout bounds, stage UI and retained last-play regression. Browser checked normal play with character defeat dialogue, enlarged opponent rows, own-score badge, and direct resume preserving the same tiles. Browser used the CSS renderer because WebGL2 is unavailable in this environment. Physical-phone GPU frame rate has not been measured.
